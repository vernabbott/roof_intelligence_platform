# Universal Conditions

Applies to every roof type.

- Ponding
- Drainage
- Debris
- Vegetation
- Flashing
- Rooftop Equipment
- Mechanical Damage
- Repairs
- Surface Contamination

## Color Variation Within One Roof Section

When the roof field, seam pattern, edges, slope, and elevation remain continuous, noticeable color variation is normally a condition cue rather than proof of a second material. Consider aging, UV weathering, chalking, dirt, repairs, moisture retention, and drainage patterns. Confirm suspected ponding only with supporting water or drainage evidence; do not infer leakage or wet insulation from discoloration alone. If weathering obscures the features needed to identify the exact material, retain the supported material-family ambiguity and reduce confidence.

## Translucent Skylight Panels on Metal Roofs

Repeated narrow rectangular panels with consistent dimensions, orientation,
spacing, and alignment within a metal roof's rib pattern may be translucent
fiberglass skylights. Treat an organized array as roof features rather than
random patches or coating variation when the imagery supports that conclusion.
For silicone restoration, exclude and protect the panel faces so they remain
light-transmitting and identifiable. Inspect their condition, perimeter seals,
and safe-access requirements onsite.
